01 · Mastery & Legacy Project

From manual scheduling to a same-day system

Problem

At an IRC education program, individualized student scheduling relied heavily on manual work — especially as new students continuously enrolled, requiring manual cross-checks of class conflicts, teacher availability, and leveling logic each time. My supervisor identified that this process was not only time-consuming but error-prone, often leaving new students without a confirmed schedule before their first class on Day 1, and asked me to redesign it.

Role & Actions

As an M&E team intern, I redesigned the leveling logic and master scheduling framework, replacing most manual cross-checking with automation (Excel pivot tables / Apps Script). This compressed scheduling turnaround from an error-prone manual process to under 30 minutes, ensuring every new student had a confirmed schedule before Day 1. I reinvested the time this freed up into more frequent, in-depth conversations with homeroom teachers, allowing individual schedules to be adjusted quickly based on real classroom performance.

Impact

— Scheduling shifted from a slow, error-prone process to a reliable, sub-30-minute turnaround — every new student was schedule-ready by Day 1
— Freed-up time was redirected into higher-frequency teacher feedback loops, making schedule adjustments more responsive to real student needs
— Built a reusable automation framework with potential to scale to other program sites — the direct basis for one of my 1-year career experiments (see below)

02 · Behavioral Agility & Resilience

Story bank

"What is your greatest strength?"

Drucker · Feedback Analysis

I expected that the growing workload from continuous new-student enrollment could only be managed manually — that speed and depth of stakeholder engagement were a trade-off. In practice, after automating the scheduling process, new students could be scheduled within 30 minutes, well before their first class on Day 1. The time this freed up went into more frequent, deeper conversations with homeroom teachers rather than more manual work.

Strength: I actively identify which parts of a workflow can be systematized, freeing myself to focus on the parts that genuinely require human judgment and listening. This reflects how I perform best — as a listener rather than a reader, and as an adviser who synthesizes input rather than pushing a single predetermined solution.

"Describe a time you dealt with conflict."

DiSC Flex

My natural DiSC profile is S-dominant (Steadiness) — I default to waiting for direction rather than initiating. During a weekly check-in cycle with my supervisor at IRC, I recognized my default style wasn't serving the project's pace, and consciously flexed toward D (Dominance): instead of waiting for her to set the week's agenda, I proactively proposed a plan — completing a first pass of the leveling logic redesign, then bringing concrete results to our next check-in. This let me take ownership of the workflow while still respecting her time.

"How do you handle a fast-paced environment?"

VUCA · GRIT

I've operated in genuinely VUCA conditions across my career — from the compressed timelines of strategy consulting at KPMG and Accenture, to leading a Post-Merger Integration program at GAP under a hard 6-month deadline, to now navigating the ambiguity of a new sector at IRC. What's kept me resilient isn't a fixed skillset — it's GRIT: staying loyal to a long-term direction while letting the specific role, sector, or approach change. The clearest test of this was my pivot from consulting into a corporate role — the hardest part wasn't learning new skills, it was unlearning old ones. The fast, decisive "consulting mode" was actually a liability in an environment built on long-term relationships and internal alignment. Growth meant showing up differently every day until the new mode became second nature.

"How do you make difficult decisions?"

Christensen · Personal Integrity

Situation

At GAP Greater China (post-acquisition by Baozun), I led the IT PMO workstream of a Post-Merger Integration program, migrating 15+ core business systems from US to local platforms within 6 months. The final three months of testing and debugging overlapped directly with Q4 preparation for major online events like Double 11, leaving almost no margin for error.

Task

The real challenge wasn't managing two workstreams at once — it was choosing to be transparent about the real risk of instability during the cutover window, rather than downplaying it to keep stakeholders comfortable.

Action

I disclosed the stability risks directly to business stakeholders ahead of time, rather than explaining them after an incident during peak sales. I coordinated with IT and the vendor to increase headcount and extend support hours, so testing and Q4 readiness could proceed in parallel rather than compete for resources. I translated technical risk into language business teams could act on, so both sides prepared together.

Result

Q4 events ran smoothly with the new system stable through the highest-pressure window. The program was completed within 6 months, generating approximately USD 400K in annualized cost savings, and established a pattern of proactive risk disclosure in how business and IT collaborated afterward.
